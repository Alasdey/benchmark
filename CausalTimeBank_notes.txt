1. WHAT IT IS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Causal-TimeBank extends the TempEval-3 / TimeBank English corpus
(183 news documents, ~1998 newswire) by adding two causal annotation
layers on top of the existing TimeML temporal annotations:

  C-SIGNAL   causal signal words inline in text
             ("because", "due to", "in exchange", "by", …)

  CLINK      directed causal link between two event instances
             eventInstanceID → relatedToEventInstance
             optionally carrying a c-signalID pointer

The rest of the TimeML structure is inherited unchanged:
  EVENT / MAKEINSTANCE  surface event mentions + instances
  TLINK                 temporal links (BEFORE, AFTER, SIMULTANEOUS, …)
  TIMEX3                time expressions

3. WHICH PAIRS WERE ANNOTATED — ANNOTATION SCOPE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The annotation was NOT exhaustive over all event pairs.
It was trigger-first and primarily sentence-local:

  Step 1 — Scan for C-SIGNALs.
    Annotators identified causal signal words in the text. Each
    C-SIGNAL triggered a CLINK annotation between the cause event
    and the effect event flanking it. This accounts for 53% of CLINKs.

  Step 2 — Annotator judgment for signal-free cases.
    The remaining 47% of CLINKs were added when causality was judged
    clear and direct even without a lexical marker.

  Scope constraint: 90% of CLINKs are within the same sentence.
    Cross-sentence causal relations were rarely annotated, even when
    present. This is a hard practical boundary on recall.

  No closed-world assumption is stated.
    Unannotated event pairs are not guaranteed to be non-causal —
    they were simply not inspected or not agreed upon. This means
    treating all non-CLINK pairs as negative examples (standard
    practice in the literature) is an approximation, not ground truth.

  No coreference layer.
    The underlying TimeBank corpus has no event coreference
    annotations. Causal-TimeBank inherits this absence — causal
    relations are never propagated through coreference chains.

  No transitivity closure.
    The 6 transitivity chains (A→B, B→C) were never closed to A→C.

  Inter-annotator agreement: κ ≈ 0.63 (reported in Mirza et al. 2014)
    Moderate agreement — signal-free CLINKs are inherently subjective.

4. HOW SOTA LITERATURE HANDLES THE SPARSITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Short answer: most papers acknowledge the sparsity, very few fix it
structurally. Nobody has re-annotated the corpus.

── 4a. Joint TLINK + CLINK modeling  [most common, ~3 papers] ──

  Mirza & Tonelli — CATENA (COLING 2016)
    Sieve-based system. TLINK classifier runs first; its output
    constraints inform the CLINK classifier. Also applies transitivity
    closure over CLINKs (adds ≤6 pairs in practice). Considered the
    baseline system for the corpus.

  Ning, Feng, Wu, Roth — ACL 2018
    Constrained conditional model (ILP) that jointly infers TLINKs
    and CLINKs. Temporal constraints (BEFORE/AFTER) prune impossible
    CLINK candidates, reducing false positives without touching the
    annotations.

  Gao et al. — NAACL 2019
    Document-level ILP with coreference propagation (requires running
    an external coref system). Joint temporal+causal+coref inference.
    Most principled structural approach in the literature.

── 4b. Data augmentation / distant supervision  [~3 papers] ──

  Zuo et al. — KnowDis (COLING 2020)
    Distant supervision using external causal knowledge bases to
    generate additional training pairs. +gain on F1 vs no augmentation.

  Zuo et al. — LearnDA (ACL 2021)
    Dual-learning loop that iteratively generates synthetic causal
    examples. +2.1 F1 on Causal-TimeBank vs prior SOTA.

  Zuo et al. — CauSeRL (ACL Findings 2021)
    Self-supervised pretraining on out-of-domain causal text before
    fine-tuning on Causal-TimeBank. +3.4 F1.
    None of these touch the annotation — they compensate externally.

── 4c. Cross-corpus transfer  [post-2022 trend] ──

  Zuo et al. — arXiv 2025
    Evaluates transfer across 6 causal datasets including
    Causal-TimeBank. Key finding: annotation diversity beats volume —
    mixing corpora (EventStoryLine, MAVEN-ERE, etc.) outperforms
    training on any one corpus alone.
    Introduces F1_phrase metric to handle annotation inconsistencies.

── 4d. LLM-era approaches  [current] ──

  Liang et al. — EMNLP 2024
    In-context contrastive learning: carefully selected positive and
    negative demonstration pairs. Implicitly handles imbalance through
    demonstration curation. No explicit discussion of sparsity.

── What nobody does ──

  • Re-annotate or extend the corpus (accepted as-is by the community)
  • Use macro-F1, PR curves, or balanced accuracy despite 21:1 imbalance
    — everyone reports standard F1 on the CLINK class only
  • Explicit negative sampling during training (surprisingly rare)
  • Transitivity closure beyond CATENA and Gao 2019

5. PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS FOR THIS PROJECT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  • The 318 CLINKs are a lower bound on true causal relations.
    Precision is probably high; recall is unknown and likely low.

  • Treating all non-CLINK pairs as negatives is standard but wrong.
    Models trained this way learn "no explicit signal nearby = no
    causality", which is a proxy for the true label.

  • Including TLINKs in the dataset (as CausalTimeBank_dataprep.py
    does) enables downstream joint reasoning — the highest-leverage
    validated technique in the literature.

  • Transitivity closure adds only 6 pairs — negligible.

  • Coreference propagation would require an external coref pass over
    the TempEval-3 text. Potentially useful but no standard version
    of this exists for the corpus.

  • For benchmarking: report F1 on CLINK class only, as all papers do,
    to stay comparable. Be aware this hides recall problems.
